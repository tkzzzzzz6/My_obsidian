# Deep Learning Based Dual-Domain CT Reconstruction

## 1. Paper Info
This is a Doctor of Engineering dissertation by Yikun Zhang at Southeast University, School of Computer Science and Engineering (June 2023).  
Keywords: dual-domain processing, deep iterative reconstruction/unrolling, GANs, sparse/limited-angle CT, and DECT.  
No DOI/arXiv is listed.

## 2. Background and Problem
Low-dose, sparse-view, and limited-angle sampling degrade measurements and introduce strong noise/artifacts.  
DECT improves material discrimination but depends on expensive dedicated hardware, limiting adoption and compatibility.

The problem is to jointly leverage projection- and image-domain information and couple deep learning with physical imaging models to recover high-quality CT/DECT images while preserving structure and data consistency.

## 3. Method Overview
The thesis presents four dual-domain tracks:  
- Low-dose CT: CLEAR (dual-domain generator + WGAN-GP).  
- Sparse-view CT: DREAM-Net (projection inpainting, residual awareness, image enhancement in an unrolled framework).  
- Sparse-view DECT: CD-Net (complementary sparse scanning + dual-stream ResUNet).  
- Limited-angle DECT: SPIE-DIR (dual-domain priors, dense cross-iteration fusion, adversarial enhancement).  

The shared idea is to enforce constraints in both projection and image spaces to reduce over-smoothing and inconsistency.

## 4. Experiments
Data: AAPM low-dose CT + external NCI/TCIA patients, real abdominal phantom and real mouse data.  
DECT uses 25 chest/abdomen patients from Nanjing Eastern Theater General Hospital, with 22 patients sampled for the limited-angle setting.  
Hardware: Ubuntu 20.04 + i7-10700K + RTX 3090; PyTorch for low-dose/sparse-view CT, TensorFlow for DECT.

Baselines:  
- Low-dose CT: FBP, TV, RED-CNN, DD-UNet, CLEAR w/o GAN.  
- Sparse-view CT: FBP, FBPConvNet, CLEAR, FISTA-Net.  
- Sparse-view DECT: PD-Net, ID-Net, SE-CD-Net, CD-Net.  
- Limited-angle DECT: FBP, DD-Net, LEARN++, CD-Net, SPIE-DIR.  

Metrics: MAE/SSIM/PSNR with visual assessment; VNC and iodine-quantification for DECT.  

Key results:  
- Low-dose CT: 15.38HU/40.10dB/97.71% (simulated) and 14.15HU/37.85dB/95.25% (1/6-dose phantom).  
- Sparse-view CT: 12.07HU/42.07dB/98.49% (simulated) and 9.28HU/37.31dB/95.63% (real mouse).  
- Sparse-view DECT: 9.39HU/44.08dB/98.99% with clearer VNC/iodine images.  
- Limited-angle DECT: 10.83HU/43.09dB/98.60% (simulated) and 10.15HU/37.74dB/96.89% (real), improved wedge artifacts and HU correction.

## 5. Contributions and Limitations
Main contributions: a cohesive dual-domain reconstruction framework across low-dose CT, sparse-view CT, sparse-view DECT, and limited-angle DECT, with CLEAR/DREAM-Net/CD-Net/SPIE-DIR as concrete instantiations and adversarial learning where appropriate.

Value: a full pipeline from acquisition design to reconstruction, validated across multiple data sources and degradation settings.

Limitations: reliance on training data distributions, limited theoretical guarantees for deep unrolling, and higher engineering complexity for real-time use.

Future work: stronger physics consistency or self-supervision, lighter global modeling, and faster inference with better cross-domain generalization.

## 6. Thoughts and Follow-ups
The dual-domain idea is portable to other inverse problems (MRI/PET), especially when separating physical operators from learned priors while keeping data consistency explicit.
