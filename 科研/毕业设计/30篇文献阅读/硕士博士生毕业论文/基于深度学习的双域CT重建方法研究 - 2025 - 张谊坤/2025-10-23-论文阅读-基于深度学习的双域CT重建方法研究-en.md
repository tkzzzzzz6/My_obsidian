# Deep Learning Based Dual-Domain CT Reconstruction

## 1. Paper Info
This is a Doctor of Engineering dissertation by Yikun Zhang at Southeast University, School of Computer Science and Engineering (June 2023). It studies dual-domain processing for CT/DECT with deep unrolling and GAN-based reconstruction; no DOI/arXiv is listed. Keywords include dual-domain processing, deep iterative reconstruction, GANs, sparse/limited-angle CT, and DECT.

## 2. Background and Problem
Low-dose, sparse-view, and limited-angle sampling degrade measurements and introduce strong noise/artifacts in reconstructions. DECT improves material discrimination but depends on expensive dedicated hardware, limiting adoption and compatibility with existing scanners.

The problem is to jointly leverage projection- and image-domain information and couple deep learning with physical imaging models to recover high-quality CT/DECT images while preserving structural details and data consistency.

## 3. Method Overview
The thesis presents four dual-domain tracks: CLEAR for low-dose CT (dual-domain generator + WGAN-GP), DREAM-Net for sparse-view CT (projection inpainting, residual awareness, image enhancement in an unrolled framework), CD-Net for sparse-view DECT (complementary sparse scanning + dual-stream ResUNet), and SPIE-DIR for limited-angle DECT (dual-domain priors, dense cross-iteration fusion, and adversarial enhancement). The shared idea is to enforce constraints in both projection and image spaces to reduce over-smoothing and inconsistency.

## 4. Experiments
Data span AAPM low-dose CT and external NCI/TCIA patients, plus real abdominal phantom and real mouse data. DECT uses 25 chest/abdomen patients from Nanjing Eastern Theater General Hospital, with 22 patients sampled for the limited-angle setting. Hardware is Ubuntu 20.04 + i7-10700K + RTX 3090; PyTorch is used for low-dose/sparse-view CT and TensorFlow for DECT chapters.

Baselines include FBP/TV/RED-CNN/DD-UNet/CLEAR w/o GAN for low-dose CT; FBP/FBPConvNet/CLEAR/FISTA-Net for sparse-view CT; PD-Net/ID-Net/SE-CD-Net/CD-Net for sparse-view DECT; and FBP/DD-Net/LEARN++/CD-Net/SPIE-DIR for limited-angle DECT. Metrics are MAE/SSIM/PSNR with visual assessment, plus VNC and iodine-quantification outcomes for DECT.

Key results: low-dose CT reaches 15.38HU/40.10dB/97.71% (simulated) and 14.15HU/37.85dB/95.25% (1/6-dose phantom); sparse-view CT reaches 12.07HU/42.07dB/98.49% (simulated) and 9.28HU/37.31dB/95.63% (real mouse). Sparse-view DECT reaches 9.39HU/44.08dB/98.99% with clearer VNC/iodine images, and limited-angle DECT reaches 10.83HU/43.09dB/98.60% (simulated) and 10.15HU/37.74dB/96.89% (real), with improved wedge artifacts and HU correction.

## 5. Contributions and Limitations
**Main contributions**: a cohesive dual-domain reconstruction framework across low-dose CT, sparse-view CT, sparse-view DECT, and limited-angle DECT, with CLEAR/DREAM-Net/CD-Net/SPIE-DIR as concrete instantiations and adversarial learning where appropriate.

**Value**: a full pipeline from acquisition design to reconstruction, validated across multiple data sources and degradation settings.

**Limitations**: reliance on training data distributions, limited theoretical guarantees for deep unrolling, and higher engineering complexity for real-time use.

**Future work**: stronger physics consistency or self-supervision, lighter global modeling, and faster inference with better cross-domain generalization.

## 6. Thoughts and Follow-ups
The dual-domain idea is portable to other inverse problems (MRI/PET) and is especially useful when separating physical operators from learned priors while keeping data consistency explicit.
